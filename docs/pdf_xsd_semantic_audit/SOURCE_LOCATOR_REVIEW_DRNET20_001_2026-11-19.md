## Authority, stronger disproof and technical scope

The official publication itself places `1000Base-X` in a **copper-based *structured cabling*** paragraph and specifies **`1 GBase-T` for CAT5e** in the directly adjacent table. IEEE 802.3 [machine-readable extracts](https://www.ieee802.org/3/publication/index.html) identify **Clause 40 as 1000BASE-T**; [historical IEEE 802.3 clause matrix](https://www.ieee802.org/3/minutes/jul00/A7_0700_StdMatrix.pdf) separates the 1000BASE-X PCS/PMA family (Clauses 36–39, including 1000BASE-CX) from 1000BASE-T (Clause 40). These sources are **terminology/context checks**, not substituted VDV normativity. In particular, **1000BASE-X is not synonymous with optical fibre**: CX is a copper variant, but does not convert the explicit `CAT5e / 1 GBase-T` copper-twisted-pair row into a 1000BASE-X PHY requirement.

**Strongest disproof attempted:** Could `1000Base-X` intentionally denote a separate media technology for high-bandwidth rear-view video, even inside the copper cable paragraph? In isolation that is logically possible because X-family includes 1000BASE-CX; no claim that every X technology requires fibre. However, the two language versions share the same *twisted-pair cable category table immediately after the paragraph*. There is no qualifier that a distinct CX/fibre physical layer is intended for CCTV, and the 1GBase-T row provides the document's explicit twisted-pair gigabit choice. Thus the **internal terminology discrepancy is supported**, while any prescriptive correction to the prose is a *likely editorial intent only*, not a new technical obligation. Never silently replace its wording or force a 1000BASE-T conformance rule.
