## Independent observations

### FR-NET20-OBS-001 — English Scope names the document as VDV 303-3

Pinned page 8 visibly begins `The VDV 303-3 describes requirements ...`. The German Scope on page 7 correctly says `VDV 301-3`, and the document footer/title identifies VDV-Schrift 301-3 throughout. This is a clear English documentation-number error, not an alternative document identity.

### FR-NET20-OBS-002 — English subsection numbering skips 2.3.4

The German section on pinned page 14 is `2.3.4 Verkabelung von Endgeräten mit Switches`. The corresponding English section on pinned page 20 is visibly numbered `2.3.5 Cabling of end devices with switches`. The English table of contents on page 6 also uses `2.3.5`, while the preceding English sequence ends at `2.3.3`. This is a bilingual section-numbering/editorial error.

### FR-NET20-OBS-003 — copper-cabling prose recommends 1000Base-X while its own table specifies 1 GBase-T

German page 10 and English page 17 are explicitly the copper-based-cable section. Their prose recommends `1000Base-X` technology for data-intensive applications, but the immediately following cable-category table uses `1 GBase-T` with CAT5e and `10 GBase-T` with CAT6A/CAT7.

This is already an internal terminology inconsistency. Public IEEE 802.3 material strengthens the interpretation: IEEE identifies Clause 40 as 1000BASE-T, and IEEE revision material explicitly distinguishes the 1000BASE-X family from the 1000BASE-T PHY over balanced copper cabling. The evidence therefore supports a likely `1000BASE-X` -> `1000BASE-T` documentation terminology error in this copper/twisted-pair context. This finding does not rewrite or substitute an external standard into the VDV document; it records the terminology conflict.

External interpretation references used for falsification:

- IEEE 802.3 public machine-readable extracts: `https://www.ieee802.org/3/publication/index.html` (Clause 40 = 1000BASE-T).
- IEEE 802.3 revision material: `https://ieee802.org/3/axay/comments/D1.0/802.3ay_D1p0_unresolved.pdf` (1000BASE-X family distinguished from 1000BASE-T; accepted-in-principle revision text).

### FR-NET20-OBS-004 — English Figure 1 caption switches safety to security

Because safety and security are distinct engineering concepts, the caption is retained as a translation-semantic documentation error rather than treated as an interchangeable synonym.

## Active falsification and rejected suspicions

1. **CAT6/55 m is not promoted as an independent technical defect.** The literal sentence `CAT 6 cables are not suitable due to the length restriction to 55m` is broad, but it immediately follows the 10GBASE-T row. Industry implementation references corroborate a 55 m reach limit for 10GBASE-T over Cat6 and 100 m for Cat6A, so the context supports a 10G-specific reading. The wording may be terse, but current evidence is insufficient for a separate defect.
2. **Figure/Table 2 and 3 naming is not promoted.** The pin-assignment layouts contain embedded connector graphics labelled Figure/Abbildung 2 and 3 while their overall captions are Table/Tabelle 2 and 3. The document's separate table and figure directories support that these are embedded figures inside tables, not necessarily a numbering contradiction.
3. **Empty version-history section is not promoted.** Page 31 contains the heading but no entries; for a first-edition publication there is no evidence that a required entry is missing.
4. **No XSD-gap finding.** VDV 301-3 is intentionally a network/physical/protocol document and is routed as such.

## Substantive network profile retained from the source

The pinned document confirms, among other things: VDV 301 recommendations are scoped to non-safety-relevant systems; coupling to safety-related networks requires feedback-free separation such as a unidirectional gateway/data diode; IEEE 802.3 is the Ethernet basis; IEEE 802.11 is referenced for WLAN; M12 is recommended for wired copper Ethernet with defined exceptions; service access may use RJ45 but should be protected from unauthorized access; vehicle networks use a star topology by default while rings are possible but not specified; Ethernet links are limited to 100 m with a practical recommendation below that in the stated test context; train and vehicle networks are separate and normally coupled via a router, with VLAN-based alternatives possible; managed switches are recommended for multicast/VLAN use and should support IGMP; PoE references IEEE 802.3af/at/bt; and vehicle interchange may use powerline, WLAN, direct Ethernet or fibre.

These retained requirements are profile knowledge only. They are not automatically converted into pass/fail rules without the later rule-authority and testability classification.
